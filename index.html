Every 10-K Speaks in Three Voices
What a company does, what it fears, and what it did — most NLP flattens them into one. We read each section separately, wire the answers into a temporal knowledge graph, and let a GNN spot which companies are quietly leaning into a theme before the market prices it. Result: 3.3× more signal, benchmark-beating portfolios across a few selected themes.

Will Small-Caps Finally Beat Large-Caps? Ask a Generative Model
The size premium is one of investing's most stubborn timing problems — decision trees look convincing in-sample, then fall apart out-of-sample. We repurpose a Conditional VAE from a detector into a directional forecaster, and stress-test which upgrades actually matter. The surprise: fancy add-ons (MoE, VampPrior) barely move the needle. What works is learning from a second market and ensembling — lifting balanced accuracy from 0.43 to 0.59 and delivering a 0.71 Sharpe rotation strategy ex-U.S. with no retraining, where no side wins automatically and timing truly pays.

A Manager Never Says "Set μ = 0.4σ"
Portfolio managers speak in sentences — "chips look better than energy, and there's real tail risk in EM FX." Portfolio tools demand exact numbers. Today a human translates by hand, losing both the nuance and the doubt. We let an LLM read each sentence into multiple plausible interpretations, each with a probability — then average what you can put a probability on, and guard against what you cannot. Entropy Pooling turns tails, rankings, and if–then views into portfolios that Black–Litterman's one-word vocabulary never could.

New Research · Paper Coming Soon
Human Sentiment Has an Expiry Date — RL Can Reset It
Human-annotated sentiment is expensive — and it quietly loses predictive power when your investment horizon changes. What sounds bearish to an analyst may not move the stock over the next month. Instead of relabeling everything, we keep the existing annotations and use reinforcement learning to re-align the sentiment reader to a chosen return horizon — the model learns to side with what the market actually did whenever the human label and realized returns clearly disagree. One labeled dataset, re-tuned to any horizon, no re-annotation.
